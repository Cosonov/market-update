Monthly Market Update · October 2026 data

Sarasota, Bradenton & Lakewood Ranch

A quick look at what's selling, what's coming on the market, and how buyers are paying across our area.

A note from Chris

October was a good reminder that our market isn't one market. Single-family homes are selling faster and closer to asking than they were a year ago, while condos are giving buyers a little more room to negotiate. Higher mortgage rates are keeping some buyers on the sidelines, but cash buyers are still very active, especially in the luxury and condo segments.

If you've been thinking about making a move this winter or spring, now is a great time to talk strategy. I'm always happy to run the numbers for your home or your neighborhood.

Chris

The short version

  • Single-family prices are up from a year ago: the median sale price rose 10.1% in Sarasota County to $490,000 and 3.7% in Manatee County to $499,000.
  • Homes are moving faster. A Sarasota single-family home went under contract in a median of 40 days, down from 61 a year ago; Manatee took 49 days.
  • Sellers are getting closer to asking: about 95.5% to 95.8% of original list price for single-family homes.
  • Inventory is tighter in Sarasota (down about 14%). Manatee single-family is the only segment with more homes for sale than last year.
  • New pending sales eased in October, so buyer activity may be cooling slightly heading into season.
  • Cash is still a big part of the market: roughly 4 in 10 Sarasota home sales and 6 in 10 condo sales were all-cash.

Market conditions

Single-family homes are sitting at about 4 months of supply, which leans slightly toward sellers. Condos and townhomes, at 5 to 5.5 months, are closer to a balanced market, with more room to negotiate.

Tip of the month

For homeowners
Bought a home this year? File for homestead by March 1.

If you own your Florida home and make it your permanent residence by January 1, you can apply for the homestead exemption for that tax year. The deadline is March 1, and filing late generally means losing the exemption for the year. It lowers your taxable value and caps how much your assessment can rise each year. File with your county property appraiser: Sarasota at sc-pa.com, Manatee at manateepao.gov. Both accept applications online.
